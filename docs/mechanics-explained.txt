IRON DIALECTIC — THE MECHANICS THE TUTORIAL DOESN'T COVER
=========================================================

The tutorial teaches the basics: the top band, factions, legitimacy, the
economy, laws, programmes, the hinterland, the clock, alerts and a first war.
This guide covers everything else that tends to confuse people, roughly in
the order you'll run into it.

Two habits help most:
  - Hover anything (or press and hold on a touch screen). Almost every number
    in the game has a tooltip that says what moves it.
  - When things go wrong, open the Report (key R). Your ministers list each
    problem, its cause, and buttons that deal with it.


PART 1 — THE NUMBERS ON THE TOP BAND
------------------------------------

TREASURY and BALANCE
  The balance (+ or − per month) counts everything that really moves money:
  taxes and the budget, but also oil, concessions, tribute paid or received,
  payments from dependencies, repairs, loans and debt repayments. Hover the
  treasury to see each item. If the treasury goes below zero you borrow
  automatically. That adds debt, and each time you borrow, inflation goes up.

INFLATION and DEBT
  Inflation falls on its own when you stop borrowing. It lowers living
  standards and business confidence. Above 60 it becomes hyperinflation, a
  crisis that hits stability. Debt has to be serviced every month. Very high
  debt can turn into a debt crisis.

INDUSTRY
  Mostly from factories, multiplied by industrial technology and the ore in
  your land. It is also the ceiling on how many arms you can make (see Kit).

FOOD and THE GRAIN YEAR
  Harvests vary: bumper, good, fair, poor or failed. Each March you get a
  forecast for the harvest that arrives in September, and a bad year makes
  the next one gentler. The Economy panel's Grain section lets you choose
  what the surplus is for:
    Export  stores 2 months; sells the surplus for gold. Best in normal years.
    Feed    stores 4 months; the population grows faster and further.
    Stock   stores a year; growth is slower, but this is the only policy that
            gets you through a failed year without imports. Full granaries
            sell part of their grain when the world price is high.
  Grain has a world price. When harvests fail worldwide, imports get
  expensive. Famine needs a bad year, empty granaries and no way to buy, and
  you always get warning.

PEOPLE
  The population grows toward a ceiling set by your land (farmland most),
  cities and the grain policy. People move with land: lose a province and
  you lose the people living there.

TROOPS
  Your uncommitted pool. It refills toward your mobilisation level (a share
  of the population, set on the War panel; the ceiling depends on your
  recruitment law). Soldiers are taken out of the workforce, so a huge army
  costs output. The Troops tooltip shows attack, defence and war weariness.

STABILITY
  Built from legitimacy plus the support of each faction, weighted by how
  much power that faction has. Unrest and war weariness cut it. The tooltip
  shows where it's heading.
    Below 38: opposition movements can rise.
    Below 33 for six months: an uprising can break out (new regimes get two
      years' grace).
    At 0 for most of a year: the state dissolves and the campaign ends.

LEGITIMACY
  Your system's right to rule. Each ideology draws on different sources
  (elections, prosperity, tradition, charisma, revolutionary fervour,
  victories...), weighted differently. The tooltip shows yours. Lose the
  source you lean on most and stability follows.

AUTHORITY
  The currency of decisions. You spend it on laws, decisions, emergency
  measures, conceding to factions and inner-circle actions. How fast it
  comes back depends on your system. When it's full, an alert tells you to
  spend it.


PART 2 — POLITICS
-----------------

FACTION POWER vs SUPPORT
  Power is how much a faction matters (from your country's structure:
  factories give workers power, a big army gives the military power, and so
  on). Support is what it thinks of you. A hostile faction with no power is
  an annoyance. A hostile faction with a quarter of the power will end you.
  "Concede" buys support for gold and authority, once every 18 months.

CENSORSHIP AND WHAT YOU'RE SHOWN
  Under a press law with censorship, the support figures you see for your own
  factions are biased. They look better than the truth. You trade accurate
  information for calm. Other countries' numbers aren't biased that way.

DRIFT
  Every regime slowly slides toward another system, driven by specific
  pressures (listed on the State panel). You can remove the causes, or
  embrace the change on your own terms before it embraces you. When a drift
  reaches 70 you get a warning dispatch.

ELECTIONS, VOTES AND THE BODY
  Systems with a parliament, congress or council make you win votes for laws
  and for offensive wars. Losing an election isn't a defeat: the country
  carries on under new management. Defensive wars never need a vote.

UNREST AND OPPOSITION MOVEMENTS
  Every province has unrest. It's raised by poor living standards, low
  stability, famine, war weariness, strikes, and being occupied rather than
  core land. Provinces can grow an opposition movement of another ideology
  (the Opposition map mode, C, shows them). Strong movements in unstable
  states can rise in revolt or start a civil war.

OCCUPIED LAND AND INTEGRATION
  Land you conquer is not core land at first. It shows an integration
  percentage. Until it reaches 100% it adds unrest and can break away or
  restore its old state. Hold it peacefully and it integrates.

THE INNER CIRCLE (REGIME PANEL, KEY I)
  Five seats: army, police, economy, ideology and foreign affairs. Each
  figure has competence, loyalty, ambition and a leaning. A capable, loyal
  figure strengthens their seat. A disloyal one works against you, and one
  who leans toward another ideology pulls your drift that way. Ambitious,
  disloyal figures plot. You can win them over, name an heir, purge, recall
  or dismiss them, which costs gold or authority. Successions go to the heir
  or the strongest figure.

SESSIONS AND LINES
  Every few years your system holds a session (party congress, imperial
  council, a new government programme after an election...). You choose one
  of three "lines": lasting modifiers plus loyalty from the figures who agree
  with it.

COUPS
  A coup is a plot that grows through three stages: grumbling, meetings, a
  date. Your security law, security budget and police chief decide how
  likely you are to find a plot at each stage. Found plots give you options:
  purge, buy off, reassign units, arrest, concede or watch. A content army
  (military support 60 or more) almost never plots on its own.
  If a coup starts, the dispatch shows the real odds and you play out four
  days of choices. Lose and you can still fight on from the provinces, go
  into exile, or serve the new regime.

DOCTRINES AND LEADERS
  Every ideology has a signature gauge (shown on the State panel) with its
  own actions, a war trait and a special war order. Mastering the doctrine
  (through its ideology programme tree) makes it stronger. Leaders carry
  traits that shift both their bonuses and how the AI behaves.

DESTINIES AND IDENTITY
  Some programmes are destinies: they rename your state, change its flag and
  leader, and open a new programme tree of their own. One destiny per state.
  If you later change your system of government, the destiny lapses and the
  old name returns. The Nation screen (click your flag in the top band) shows
  your motto, anthem, epithets and history.

EMERGENCY MEASURES
  Short programmes that appear when a specific trouble hits (bankruptcy,
  famine, a failing front...). Starting one shelves your current programme,
  which resumes when the measure finishes.


PART 3 — WAR, BEYOND THE BASICS
-------------------------------

SIZE MATTERS BOTH WAYS
  A sprawling state (over 7% of the world's land) is overstretched: less
  output, weaker attacks, more unrest. A small one (under 3.5%) runs well:
  better efficiency, science, trade and defence. A much weaker defender
  fights as a nation in arms and defends up to 35% better.

WHAT YOU COMMIT CAN'T DEFEND
  Troops you commit to an attack leave the pool. An empty pool makes every
  one of your tiles cheap to take. Keep a reserve.

KIT (EQUIPMENT)
  Kit 100% means as much equipment per soldier as the world's average army.
  It multiplies attack (and defence a little less). Your army draws on an
  arsenal, which fills from the arms budget (capped by your factories),
  arms bought from friendly partners, and depots captured with enemy cities
  and factories. War drains it: every month you fight, every soldier lost,
  every barrage. Fill the arsenal before a war, and bomb the enemy's
  factories so theirs can't refill.

WAR WEARINESS
  Rises while you fight and lose people. It cuts stability and living
  standards, and above about 55 you'll be offered peace feelers. It falls
  faster in peace.

POSTURE
  Offensive, Balanced, Dig in or Elastic defence, on the war bar. An elastic
  defence gives ground slowly and makes every enemy tile expensive.
  Changing posture costs 20 days of reorganisation.

ATTACK STYLES
  Each style trades speed for losses. Siege ignores most of a fortress,
  infiltration ignores half of it. Some styles are only allowed under some
  systems. The War page of the Handbook lists them all.

OBJECTIVES
  Select an enemy city or capital and press "Make this the objective". The
  front becomes a spearhead toward it: 10% faster, a little costlier on its
  flanks, and good at cutting off pockets. Taking it costs the enemy troops,
  stability and morale, and your attack surges for three weeks.

SUPPLY, POCKETS AND ENCIRCLEMENT
  The Supply map (B) shows three states: supplied by land (joined to your
  capital), supplied by sea (through a port or a beachhead), and cut off.
  Cut-off land with an enemy next to it surrenders within about 40 days,
  along with the troops in it. Encircled pockets are outlined in red with a
  countdown. Prisoners are held until the peace, and six in ten come home.

FORTRESSES
  A fort covers every tile within 6. At full condition attackers there
  advance much slower and lose far more men. Shelling and bombing also kill
  fewer troops under fort cover.

BUILDING CONDITION
  Every building has a condition from 0 to 100%, and what it does scales with
  it. Land taken in war damages its buildings; artillery, bombing, missiles
  and the bomb damage more. Repairs are automatic and paid from the treasury,
  slower at war. A wreck (0%) does nothing until rebuilt for half its price.

LANDINGS AND SETTLING
  Naval landings need a port. Select an enemy coast and choose a landing.
  Sea power (ports, naval programmes, technology) decides how much of the
  convoy survives. Empty islands can be settled: select an unclaimed coast
  and press Settle. The button shows the cost and travel time.

ARTILLERY, BOMBING AND THE BOMB
  Barrages and bombing raids cost gold and have cooldowns. They kill troops
  in a radius and damage buildings (bombing is best against factories and
  ports). The atomic programme needs military technology, working factories,
  and a share of your revenue for years. Other countries' intelligence learns
  of it halfway through. Warheads need silos. A bomb destroys everything in
  its radius and poisons the land for years.

CAPITULATION AND PEACE TERMS
  A state that loses its capital and two thirds of its land capitulates: you
  can annex it, install your system, or take an indemnity. Negotiated peace
  terms depend on the winner's ideology family:
    Liberal        reparations, and bases if you won clearly
    Conservative   tribute: a share of the loser's revenue for ten years
    Socialist / anarchist  your movement is planted in the loser
    others         land only
  After a peace, a truce lasts eight years.

THREAT AND COALITIONS
  Wars of conquest, especially without a casus belli, raise your threat.
  High threat lets others declare war on you with a "containment" or
  "coalition" justification, and coalitions form against the runaway power.


PART 4 — THE WORLD AROUND YOU
-----------------------------

OPINION, TREATIES AND BLOCS
  Opinion runs from −100 to +100 and is driven mostly by how close your
  ideologies are. Trade treaties pay both sides; the route's value depends on
  distance, ports and ideological fit (planned and market economies trade
  less well together). Blocs call their members to defend each other.

DEPENDENCIES AND SOVEREIGNTY (KEY U)
  A dependency (vilayet, protectorate, mandate, treaty state, client) pays
  its overlord a share of revenue, and the overlord can veto its wars and
  laws. Resistance grows over time. At 80 or more the country can rise. You
  can also petition for a treaty, or wait for the overlord to weaken.

OIL
  Oil fields are found over time and pay well, especially after the oil shock
  of the 1970s. Foreign companies may hold concessions. Nationalising them
  angers the company's home state, which may accept compensation, embargo
  you, plot a coup or invade.

THE SLUMP
  Somewhere around 1927 to 1933 a world depression hits. Trade falls,
  business confidence collapses and market economies suffer most. Individual
  countries can also fall into their own slumps when confidence is low.

FORTUNE
  The game avoids long runs of bad news. After a string of disasters, good
  dispatches become more likely.


PART 5 — DISPATCHES, REPORTS AND THE PAPER
------------------------------------------

THE DISPATCH TRAY
  Urgent dispatches (crises, history) stop the clock. Ordinary ones wait at
  the left of the map while time runs. Leave one for three months and your
  ministers settle it for you. You can make every dispatch stop the clock in
  Settings.

THE REPORT (R)
  Ranked problems with their causes and one-click fixes. Urgent items light
  up the Report tab and add an alert.

THE WORLD DISPATCH
  A newspaper with stories from other countries: revolutions, wars, coups,
  new programmes. It's the easiest way to notice a neighbour turning hostile.
  Settings decide how often it pops up.

PROPAGANDA BANNERS
  Under an absolute monarch or a personal dictator, a banner of slogans
  sweeps across the map now and then, more often as the cult grows. You can
  switch it off in Settings.


PART 6 — WINNING AND LOSING
---------------------------

  You win by:
    Domination    holding 55% of the world's land
    Ideological   60% of the world's people under your ideology's family,
                  with you as its largest state
    Hegemony      35% of world output plus trade treaties with most of the
                  world, held for five years
    Grand objective  each ideology has its own (see the Handbook)
  If nobody has won by 1976, history gives a verdict by score.

  A victory doesn't end the game. You can keep playing past it, past 1976,
  and past a coup or revolution that leaves your country alive.

  You lose when your state is conquered, a coup you resisted succeeds and
  you don't fight on, you lose a civil war, or stability sits at zero for
  most of a year and the country falls apart.
