IRON DIALECTIC — WHAT'S NEW: YOUR COUNTRIES, YOUR MAPS
=======================================================

This update adds four things: country customization, the map maker, map codes
for sharing, and two new map sizes (including a World map). Everything below
works with a mouse or trackpad and on phones and tablets.


1. CUSTOMIZE ANY COUNTRY
------------------------
On the New campaign screen, press "Customize this country" under the state you
picked, or open "Other countries" and press Customize next to anyone else.

What you can change:
  - Name, adjective and full state name. Leave the full name blank and it
    follows the system of government, as before ("Republic of X", "Kingdom
    of X"). A full name you write stays until the country changes its system.
  - Capital name and the first leader's name. Later leaders are picked the
    usual way.
  - Map colour, from a palette that stays readable on the map. If the colour
    is too close to a neighbour's, the editor warns you.
  - Flag. Three choices:
      The game's flag: drawn from the country's colour and system, and it
      changes when history changes the country.
      Build one: pick a layout (stripes, cross, canton, saltire and more),
      up to four colours and an emblem.
      Upload: any PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF. It's cropped to 3:2 and shrunk to
      60×40, so it stays small.
    A custom flag shows up everywhere: the map labels, the top band,
    dispatches, The World Dispatch, the panels and the end screens.
    "Let history change the flag": tick this if you want a destiny or a
    revolution to replace your flag with its own. Leave it off and your flag
    stays no matter what.
  - Motto, anthem title and a short description. Blank fields use the
    game's own text.
  - Starting situation (great power, sick empire, rich and divided, small and
    threatened, agrarian backwater, young revolution, trading state, middle
    power) and starting system of government.

Renaming keeps a country's content. Rename Aurelia to "Bobland" and it still
gets Aurelia's programmes, destinies and history, and they now say Bobland.

Presets: "Save as a preset" keeps a country design in your browser so you can
reuse it in later campaigns.

"Restore history" puts every country back exactly as it was. Challenges always
use the original countries.


2. THE MAP MAKER
----------------
Open it from the title screen. You can start from a preset or from one of
your saved maps.

Presets:
  - An empty sea (two islands; you paint everything else)
  - World map (new: the continents of a globe, with 30 states)
  - The Three Continents (the 1910 campaign map, as an editable copy)
  - A random world
  - Pangaea, Archipelago, Two rival continents, The inland sea,
    The island empire

The panel has six tabs. Each one says at the top what it's for, and the
dark line along the bottom of the map always says what your next click or
drag will do and what's under the cursor.

  SCENARIO  Name, start year (1910, 1925 or 1940), map size, two switches
            for what the game fills in automatically, a list of problems to
            fix before play, and saving and sharing.

  TERRAIN   Paint sea, plains, farmland, forest, hills, mountain and desert,
            and add or erase rivers. Terrain decides food, population and
            how hard the land is to attack.

  BORDERS   Paint which state owns each tile. Painted land belongs to that
            state at the start, exactly as drawn. "Nobody" paints unclaimed
            hinterland, and "Erase" removes painted borders.
            Switch: "States grow into land you have not painted". On, states
            spread from their capitals into unpainted land. Off, unpainted
            land belongs to nobody (except each capital's own province).
            "Lock in the borders you see" turns the current borders into
            painted ones so you can adjust them tile by tile.

  STATES    Add a state (click "Add a state", then click land for its
            capital), drag a capital to move it, and edit a state:
              Edit country... opens the full country editor (name, flag,
                colour, system, situation, leader, motto...)
              Growth: how far it spreads into unpainted land
              Treasury and Stability: set a starting value, or leave them
                on "default" so the game decides
              Alliance: states in the same alliance start in one bloc
              Overlord: the state starts as a dependency of another (a
                protectorate, vilayet, mandate, treaty state or client)

  BUILDINGS Place cities, factories, ports (coast only) and forts on exact
            tiles. Switch: "Also add the usual cities, factories, ports and
            forts". Turn it off and only your buildings exist (plus a city at
            each capital). "Lock in the buildings you see" turns the
            automatic ones into placed ones you can remove one by one.

  GENERATOR The generator that made the starting land. It's locked so
            nothing reshapes your map by accident. Unlock it to move, resize,
            add or delete continents, change the sea level or roll new
            coastlines. Your painting always stays on top. You can also turn
            the generator off for good and keep the land as painting.

Tools (Terrain and Borders tabs):
  Brush  drag to paint; sizes from 1 to 25 tiles across. A red preview shows
         exactly which tiles the next click changes.
  Fill   click once to fill a whole connected area of the same kind.
  Pick   click a tile to copy its terrain or owner into the brush.

Keys: Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, B brush, F fill, I pick, [ and ] brush size.
On a touch screen, one finger paints and two fingers move and zoom.

Painting is local. Painting a coastline or a mountain range changes the
provinces, rivers and borders right there and nowhere else.

Before you play, the Scenario tab lists problems. Red ones stop play (for
example, too little land for every state). Yellow ones are warnings: a
capital at sea, a state with very little land, lone specks of land.

Programmes and destinies on custom maps: the countries of the Three
Continents keep their own programme trees and destinies wherever you put
them, even renamed. Every other state gets the family and ideology
programmes, as on random worlds. The scripted history (the great war of 1914
and so on) belongs to the Three Continents campaign only.

"Play this map" takes you to the New campaign screen with your map selected.
Pick a state and begin. Saved games carry the map inside them, so they load
without the map code.


3. SHARING MAPS: CODES AND FILES
--------------------------------
  - Copy code: in the map maker (Share) and on the New campaign screen. It
    gives you a code starting with IDM1- that contains the whole map: land,
    borders, buildings, states, flags and options.
  - Load code: on the title screen and in the map maker. Paste a code, then
    "Play it" or "Open in the map maker". A damaged or cut-off code shows an
    error instead of crashing.
  - Codes from other players are checked before they load: names are
    cleaned, numbers are kept within limits, and only PNG or WebP flag images
    are accepted.
  - Code length: a preset is about 1,000 to 3,000 characters. Uploaded flags
    and painted borders make codes longer. A fully painted world can run to
    15,000 to 30,000 characters. You can leave uploaded flags out of a code,
    or use Export file / Import file (.idmap) for big maps.
  - Copy code on the New campaign screen also works for the Three Continents
    and random worlds, with your country customizations included.


4. NEW MAP SIZES
----------------
  - Enormous: 640×360 tiles, 32 states. Available for random worlds and in
    the map maker. It's nearly three times the standard map, so campaigns are
    longer and older computers may run a bit slower.
  - World: 720×288, a wide map in the proportions of a globe laid flat. Used
    by the World map preset and available in the map maker.


5. ON THE TITLE SCREEN
----------------------
Map maker is now one of the main buttons, Load code sits with the smaller
buttons, and there's a "Join the Discord" button.
